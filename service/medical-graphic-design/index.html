Medical branding and graphic design

Logos, brochures, postcards and print materials that give your practice one consistent, professional image.

In plain words

Medical branding and graphic design is the work of creating your practice's visual identity: an original logo, a set of colors and typefaces, and the printed pieces patients actually hold, such as brochures and postcards. A practice needs it because patients judge professionalism by what they see, and mismatched materials from different vendors make a good practice look disorganized. We design and write everything as one matching set, so your front desk handouts, mailers and social media graphics all look like they come from the same place.

What you get ℞

Original logo files

A logo designed only for your practice, delivered in the formats needed for signs, print, your website and social media.

Brand style guide

A short document listing your exact colors, typefaces and logo rules so anyone producing materials for you keeps them consistent.

Practice brochure

A custom brochure covering your credentials, background and services, written by our staff and designed with your logo and practice details.

Patient information brochures

Single-topic brochures that explain one service to interested patients, with text our physician staff can write and you approve.

Referral brochures

Handouts your staff give to patients to pass along to friends and family, describing your credentials, services and current promotions.

Services postcard and extras

A 6×9 postcard listing your services and contact details, plus stationery, business cards, posters, displays or social media graphics as needed.

This is a good fit if

  • You are opening a new practice or location and need a logo and printed materials from scratch
  • Your current brochures, cards and signs were made at different times and no longer match each other
  • You want to encourage patient referrals and need something professional for staff to hand out

How it works

From the first conversation to ongoing results.

  1. Discovery conversation

    We ask about your specialty, your patients, your budget and the look you like or dislike. You send us any existing logo, photos, provider bios and a list of the services you want to feature.

  2. Logo and identity

    Our designers present logo options with matching colors and type, and you tell us what to keep and what to change. Once you approve a direction, it becomes the basis for every other piece.

  3. Writing and layout

    We write the brochure and postcard text, lay out each piece and send you proofs to review. You check names, credentials, services, promotions and contact details for accuracy before anything is finalized.

  4. Files and updates

    We deliver final files ready for a print shop and for online use, organized so your staff can find them. When you add a provider, a service or a promotion, we update the affected pieces in the same style.

The tools involved, explained

What each one is, and how we use it for your practice.

Vector design software

Professional drawing programs that build a logo from shapes instead of pixels, so it stays sharp at any size from a business card to an outdoor sign. Our designers use them to create your logo and lay out your printed pieces.

AI language and image models

Software that can draft text and suggest visual ideas quickly. We use it to assist with first drafts and early concepts, then our designers and writers refine the work, and physicians review any medical content before you see it.

Print-ready PDF files

The standard file format print shops accept, with the correct size, margins and color settings built in. We prepare one for each piece so you can send it to the printer of your choice without extra setup.

Shared brand folder

An online folder that holds your logo files, style guide and templates in one place. We organize it so your staff, your printer and any future vendor all work from the same approved versions.

Words you will hear

Brand identity
The combination of logo, colors, typefaces and layout style that makes your practice recognizable wherever it appears.
Proof
A preview of a design sent for your review and approval before it is printed or published.
Bleed
A small extra margin of color or image beyond the edge of a printed piece, trimmed off so no white border shows.

Questions we hear

We already have a logo. Can you work with it, or do we have to start over?

You do not have to start over. Our designers can build your brochures, postcards and other materials around your current logo and practice details, and we can set the colors, type and templates that keep everything consistent. If your logo no longer fits the look of your practice, we can create an original one as part of the project.

What do we need to provide to get a brochure or postcard started?

We need the basics about your practice: your credentials and background, the services you offer, your address and phone number, and any special promotions you want to feature. Our staff writes the copy about your background and services, and our physician staff can write patient information material on individual services. You review the drafts for accuracy before anything goes to print.

How much does a branding or print project cost, and how long does it take?

Both depend on the practice and on what you need, since a single services postcard is a very different project from a new logo with a full set of brochures, stationery and displays. We start with a free analysis of your practice and then give you a quote and a plan that fits your budget. Review rounds and how quickly you approve drafts are usually the biggest factors in timing.

How do we keep everything looking consistent across print, our office and social media?

Consistency comes from deciding on the logo, colors, type and templates first, then applying them to every piece you publish. We use that same identity for your brochures, referral pieces, 6x9 services postcards, posters, table top and seminar displays, invitations, stationery, business cards, and graphics for social media and ads. The common thing to watch out for is ordering pieces one at a time from different sources, which is how a practice's image starts to drift.
