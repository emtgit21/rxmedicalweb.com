What makes this specialty different to market.

We start from how your patients decide, not from a list of services.

  1. Two audiences, one practice

    Referring physicians want to know your training, your procedures and how quickly you see their patients, while patients want to know whether you treat their condition and take their insurance. Most pain practice websites speak to neither group clearly.

  2. Advertising platforms treat pain cautiously

    Ad platforms and regulators look closely at anything that touches prescription medication, opioids or claims of relief, and regenerative treatments draw extra attention. Campaigns written without that in mind get disapproved, or attract patients who are not a fit for an interventional practice.

  3. Older patients and insurance questions

    Many of your patients are older, call instead of filling out forms, and want to confirm coverage before they book. If the phone goes unanswered or the site is hard to read, they move on to the next practice on the list.

Advertising with care

  • Advertising that mentions prescription medication, opioids or controlled substances is restricted by ad platforms and regulators, so we build campaigns around conditions and procedures with these limits in mind, and the practice should confirm specifics with its counsel.
  • Claims about pain relief, results or avoiding surgery, and any description of regenerative treatments, need careful and supportable wording, so we write without promises of outcome and the practice confirms final language with its counsel or compliance contact.
  • Testimonials, patient stories and replies to reviews can reveal health information and usually call for written patient authorization, so we build review and testimonial work with HIPAA requirements in mind and the practice confirms its consent process with counsel.

Questions we hear from pain management

Most of our patients come from physician referrals. Can marketing help with that?

Yes. We make it easy for a referring office to see who you are, what you treat and how to send a patient, and we can produce print and e-mail materials for the offices you want to reach. Patient-facing marketing then adds a second source of new patients alongside those referrals.

How do you keep our ads from attracting people who only want medication?

We write campaigns around conditions and interventional procedures, exclude medication-related searches, and state clearly on the landing pages what your practice does and does not offer. We cannot filter every inquiry, but the wording and targeting shape who calls, and our AI agents and intake forms can ask screening questions you approve.

Can we market regenerative treatments?

You can present them as services your practice offers, but this is an area where regulators and ad platforms pay close attention to claims. We describe what the treatment and visit involve without stating what it will do for a patient, and we ask that your counsel review the final wording.

Who writes the medical content, and do you use AI?

AI assists with research and first drafts, and physicians review the medical content before anything goes live. You see and approve every page, and nothing is published in your name without your sign-off.
