Rx for dental practices

Marketing for general, cosmetic and implant dental practices

Dental patients choose from a short list of nearby offices, and they ask about insurance before anything else. We market for both sides of your schedule: steady hygiene recall and the implant and cosmetic cases that take longer to decide.

What makes this specialty different to market.

We start from how your patients decide, not from a list of services.

  1. Many offices, one small map

    Most patients pick a dentist from the few practices that appear in the map results for their neighborhood, and several offices near you are competing for those same spots. Your reviews, your Google profile and the accuracy of your listings carry as much weight as your website.

  2. Insurance questions come first

    New patients want to know whether you take their plan before they read anything about your dentists or your technology. If the website and the front desk cannot answer that quickly and clearly, the patient calls the next office on the list.

  3. Two very different patient decisions

    A cleaning or a toothache is booked within minutes, while implants, veneers and aligners are researched for weeks and compared across several offices. One message and one budget rarely serve both, so high-value cases get lost when everything is marketed like a new patient special.

Advertising with care

  • Before-and-after photos of smile makeovers, implants and whitening need written patient consent and should show typical cases without implying every patient will see the same result, and we build campaigns with this in mind.
  • Many state dental boards have rules on advertising specialty credentials, terms such as "specialist" or "painless," and how discounts, free exams and financing offers are disclosed, so the practice should confirm its wording with its counsel or compliance contact.
  • Replies to reviews and use of testimonials must never confirm that someone is a patient or reveal treatment details without authorization, and we write replies and collect testimonials with HIPAA requirements in mind.

Questions we hear from dental practices

We already get most new patients from insurance directories. What would you add?

Plan directories send patients who are choosing by network, and they still look at your reviews and website before calling. We work on the map results, your reputation and your site so more of those patients choose you, and we build demand for implant, cosmetic and other cases that directories do not bring in.

Can you connect online booking to our practice management software?

In many cases, yes. We connect through the APIs and integrations your software vendor makes available, and where a direct connection is not possible we set up a booking request flow that your front desk confirms. We check what your system supports before we promise a specific setup.

How do you market implants and cosmetic cases differently from general dentistry?

These patients research longer, compare offices and worry about cost, so we give each procedure its own page, landing page and follow-up sequence. Leads are tracked from the first inquiry to the consultation, so your team knows who still needs a call back.

Who writes the content about dental procedures?

AI assists with drafts, and our in-house writers and clinical reviewers edit every page before it goes to you for approval. Nothing is published about your procedures, credentials or offers until your dentist has signed off.
